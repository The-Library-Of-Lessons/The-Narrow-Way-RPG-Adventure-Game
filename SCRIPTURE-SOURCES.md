# Scripture and the fictional journey

The story uses fictional people and settings. Dialogue attributed to Mara, Eli, Jonah, and the guardians is original fiction. It is not presented as speech by Jesus or as additional Scripture.

The journal quotes the King James Version and gives context and a source link for each passage:

- [Luke 10:37](https://biblehub.com/kjv/luke/10-37.htm): Jesus' closing instruction in the Good Samaritan account. Suggested reading: Luke 10:25–37.
- [Mark 12:31](https://biblehub.com/kjv/mark/12-31.htm): love of neighbor in the discussion of the greatest commandments. Suggested reading: Mark 12:28–34, including love for God.
- [James 1:19](https://biblehub.com/kjv/james/1-19.htm): instruction in the letter of James, not a direct quotation of Jesus. Suggested reading: James 1:19–27.
- [Luke 19:8](https://biblehub.com/kjv/luke/19-8.htm): Zacchaeus' response in his encounter with Jesus. The journal distinguishes this from a universal repayment formula commanded by Jesus. Suggested reading: Luke 19:1–10.
- [Matthew 5:44](https://biblehub.com/kjv/matthew/5-44.htm): Jesus' teaching on love for enemies. Suggested reading: Matthew 5:43–48.
- [John 13:34](https://biblehub.com/kjv/john/13-34.htm): Jesus' command to love one another as he has loved his disciples. Suggested reading: John 13, including the washing of feet.
- [Matthew 22:37–40](https://biblehub.com/kjv/matthew/22.htm): Jesus identifies love for God with the whole heart, soul, and mind, and love for one's neighbor as oneself. The expansion quotes both commandments together; its fictional dilemmas do not claim a new divine command or a guaranteed painless outcome.

The existing Willowbrook chapter also includes Romans 12:21 and 1 John 3:18, labeled KJV. The sanctuary basin discovery directs players to John 13:1–17 without inventing a quotation.

## Learning design

The journal records seven discoveries rather than assigning a spiritual rank. Optional questions give explanatory feedback and can be revisited without a penalty. Completion does not claim to measure the player's love, wisdom, faith, or standing before God.

Practical themes—sharing resources, checking a report, repairing harm, and providing supervised aid—are applications invented for this story. The contextual notes encourage reading the Bible passage itself. Decisions have relational consequences in later dialogue, but no answer earns divine favor or a moral currency.

Review with a trusted biblical educator and playtest with the intended audience before a public release. The goal is understanding and thoughtful practice, not replacing Scripture or pastoral teaching with game dialogue.
